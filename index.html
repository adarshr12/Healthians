The Problem

Why getting a health test in India is hard

Two fundamental problems that Healthians was built to solve.

🧪

You can't always trust the results

Most diagnostic labs in India are not officially certified. There is no easy way for a patient to know if their test results are accurate. This is not rare — it is the default.

🕐

Getting a test wastes your day

You take time off work, travel to a lab, wait in a queue, and come back for the report. For someone who needs tests every 3 months, or an elderly person who cannot travel, this system doesn't fit their life.

User Research

What People Struggle With

The everyday frustrations people face when trying to get a health test or see a doctor in India — and how Healthians addresses them.

📞

Calling hospitals to check availability

The common way to book a test is to call a lab or hospital, check if a slot is free, and then call again to confirm. It is slow and depends on someone picking up the phone.

Healthians addresses this — book in the app in a few taps
🚗

Travelling to a lab and waiting in a queue

Getting a blood test done means taking time off, travelling to the lab, and often waiting. For a fasting test at 7 AM, this means leaving home early on an empty stomach.

Healthians addresses this — sample collector comes to your home
📋

No single place for health records

Past test reports are scattered — some on paper, some on WhatsApp, some physically at the lab. When a doctor asks for last month's result, most people can't find it easily.

Healthians addresses this — all reports saved in the app
🩺

No easy way to talk to a doctor quickly

If you're worried about a symptom, calling a clinic and getting an appointment takes time. People often wait days to see a doctor for something that could be addressed in a short call.

Healthians addresses this — tele-consultation from ₹129
💰

Not knowing what a test will cost

Test prices vary between labs and are rarely shown upfront. People often find out the cost only when they get there, which creates uncertainty — especially for people on a budget.

Healthians shows prices upfront with discounts applied
📄

Not understanding what the test report means

Lab reports show numbers alongside reference ranges, but most people don't know what a slightly elevated TSH or low Vitamin D actually means for their health.

Partially addressed — AI report explainer launched Aug 2024
🔁

Forgetting to rebook a repeat test

A doctor says "come back in 3 months for the same test." Three months pass, the patient forgets, or keeps putting it off. This is very common for people managing long-term conditions.

Partially addressed — reminders exist but personalised rebook nudges are limited
🏥

Not knowing if a doctor or lab is trustworthy

When someone is new to a city, they don't know which doctor to go to or whether a lab is certified. They usually ask a friend or search online and get too many options with no clear guidance.

Partially addressed — Healthians uses certified labs, but doctor profiles lack visible credentials or ratings
📅

Not preparing correctly before a test

Some tests need fasting. Some need you to stop certain medicines. Most people only find this out when they reach the lab — and sometimes have to come back another day.

Partially addressed — WhatsApp reminders are sent, but pre-test preparation instructions are not prominently surfaced

Section 07

How Healthians Works

Two ways people get tested: directly through the app, or through a local partner who earns a commission on each test.

Direct — Patient Books via App

Someone downloads the app, searches for a test, books a slot, pays, and gets the result in the app. Healthians' own sample collectors do the home visit.

Through Partners — Doctors, Clinics, Local Operators

Partners register at healthians.com/franchisee. They book tests for their patients, collect the sample using Healthians' kit, and earn commission per completed report. Healthians provides:

  • Free sample collection materials and kits
  • Technology platform + training
  • Dedicated support person
  • Branding and marketing materials
  • Logistics network for sample transport
  • Wide test catalogue across 10+ diagnostic fields

Onboarding takes 7 days. Nominal fee + documents required. Source: healthians.com/franchisee

Section 16

App Metrics

Five categories. For each: what it is, how to calculate it, and what it means for a low-frequency health app like Healthians.

📥 Acquisition
Cost Per Install (CPI) / Cost to Acquire a Customer (CAC)
CPI = Total marketing spend ÷ New users acquired
How much you spend to get one new user. CPI alone means nothing — it must be compared against how much that user will spend over time.
For Healthians: Break by channel. A user from Google Search ("thyroid test at home") is far more likely to book than one from a broad Instagram ad. High CPI from search can still be profitable.
Customer Lifetime Value (CLTV)
Avg booking value × Avg bookings per year × Avg years as customer
Total money you expect from one customer across all their bookings, for as long as they use Healthians.
For Healthians: A chronic patient booking thyroid every 3 months at ₹427 = roughly ₹5,100 over 3 years. A one-time sick user = ₹200–500. Converting sick users into regular health checkers is the highest-value product problem to solve.
💬 Engagement
DAU / MAU
DAU = unique users per day · MAU = unique users in a month
Raw DAU is almost useless for a health test app. Nobody opens a diagnostics app every day.
Track actioned MAU — users who booked, logged a vital, or viewed a report. Split into: Revenue MAU (booked) vs Engagement MAU (logged or viewed) vs Passive MAU (just opened).
Stickiness Ratio
DAU ÷ MAU × 100
What % of monthly users come back on any given day. Social apps target 50%+. For Healthians, 5–10% is strong given the low booking frequency.
The real stickiness signal: do users open the app between bookings — for vitals logging, health score, or report review? Near-zero stickiness means nothing is pulling users back between test cycles.
Session Length
Time spent in app per visit
Must be measured separately by session type. Mixing them gives the wrong direction.
Booking sessions should be short (fast = good). Care and report sessions should be long (more time = more engaged). One average number hides both.
Churn Rate
% of previously active users who have stopped using the app
Cannot use 30-day churn here — it would misclassify healthy customers as churned.
Define churned as: no session in 9+ months AND no upcoming booking. A chronic patient with no rebook in 180+ days is at-risk. They haven't stopped needing the test — they've just forgotten Healthians.
Exit Rate
% of sessions ending on each screen
Tells you exactly where users leave the app.
Watch: slot screen (no suitable time = exit), coupon/payment screen (price confusion = exit), report screen (hard to read = switch to WhatsApp PDF), home screen first visit (unclear = immediate exit).
🎯 Conversion
Goal Completion Rate
Users who completed a flow ÷ Users who started it × 100
% of users who started a key action and finished it.
Key goals: booking completed, report opened in-app (not just WhatsApp), consultation booked post-report, family member added, vital logged.
Retention Rate
(Users at end − New users) ÷ Users at start × 100
Standard D1/D7/D30 windows are wrong for Healthians. The right windows depend on user type.
Sick User90 days — did they come back at all?
Chronic Patient3 months + 6 months — rebooking on schedule?
Health-Conscious6 months — back for second checkup?
All users9 months — broad loyalty signal
Aha Moment
The moment a new user first understands the value of the product
Everything before this moment should be fast and clear. Everything after should build habits and repeat use.
For Healthians: Getting a readable, labelled report (Normal / High / Low) in the app within 24 hours — without leaving home. That's when the user thinks "this actually works." Secondary Aha: booking for a family member (it's a household tool), seeing a health trend across multiple tests (it's a health record).
💰 Monetary
Average Order Value (AOV)
Total revenue ÷ Number of bookings
Average spend per booking. Has a natural ceiling — growing AOV means more tests per booking, not higher prices.
AOV levers: packages vs single tests, family bookings in one session, consultation added after a result. More important alongside AOV: Annual Revenue per User — total spend per person across 12 months.
Revenue
Track as: monthly total · by user type · by service type
Three cuts that matter most.
Monthly trend shows overall growth. By user type (chronic vs health-conscious vs sick) shows which segment drives value. By service (blood tests vs scans vs consultations) shows where to invest product effort next.
⚡ App Performance
App Load Time
How long it takes to open the app. Slow load = users leave before seeing anything.
Tool: Firebase Performance
App Crashes
How often the app closes on its own. A crash during payment = lost booking + user doesn't know if they were charged.
Tool: Firebase Crashlytics
App Latency
How long server requests take — loading slots, processing payment, showing the report. Slowness at these three moments kills conversion.
Tool: Firebase custom trace

🚫 Vanity Metrics — What NOT to Optimise For

What is a vanity metric? A number that looks impressive but doesn't actually tell you whether the product or business is healthy. It can go up while real performance goes down.

These four are commonly tracked but should not be used as primary success metrics for Healthians.

Total App Downloads
Tells you nothing about whether anyone booked. 50L installs with 3% conversion is worse than 10L installs with 20% conversion.
Number of Registered Users
Includes everyone who signed up once and never came back. Looks big, means little without an activation rate alongside it.
Screens Per Visit
A user bouncing in confusion looks identical to one exploring thoughtfully. The number alone tells you nothing about intent.
Daily Sessions per DAU
Can be inflated by frustrated users opening and closing the app repeatedly. High sessions do not mean high engagement.

Section 20

App Store Dynamics

The app store is not just a download channel — it is a public feedback loop, a ranking signal, and a trust indicator.

📱 How Store Ranking Works

Google Play and the App Store rank apps based on signals that a PM can directly influence through product decisions.

Crash rate — flagged above 1.09% App freeze rate — flagged above 0.47% Review rating and volume Install-to-uninstall speed Session length and return rate
An app with a 1.5% crash rate can lose store visibility automatically — Google Play reduces search ranking without any human review. Source: Google Play Console, Android Vitals.

⭐ Reviews as a Product Signal

App store reviews surface user frustration faster than most internal metrics. They show up before support tickets and often identify friction before it shows in numbers.

Specific screen where users get stuck Sample collector punctuality concerns Report delivery delays Coupon codes not applying
Recurring themes in public reviews and complaint platforms (Voxya, ConsumerComplaints.in): sample collector delays, report turnaround, and coupon frustration. Source: Voxya.com, ConsumerComplaints.in — reviewed May 2026.

🔍 App Store Optimisation (ASO)

Making the app easier to find when someone searches in the Play Store or App Store. For Healthians, the most valuable searches are intent-driven — someone searching for a specific test.

Title — "Healthians: Blood Test at Home" Keywords — thyroid test, HbA1c, full body, dengue test Screenshots — show the booking flow, not just the logo Rating prompt timing — ask after a successful report, not during booking

🏥 Health App Store Rules

Health apps have additional constraints.

Google Play Health Policy: Apps collecting health data must declare usage explicitly. Test results, vitals, and blood sugar readings are sensitive data. Privacy policy must reflect this.

Rating prompt timing: If a user just got an abnormal result, asking for a rating at that moment will produce a low score. The right moment is after they view a normal result successfully.

The Three Moments That Cannot Fail

💳 Payment Screen Crash

User has committed to buying. A crash = lost booking + they don't know if they were charged. Trust broken immediately.

🕐 Slot Screen Freeze

Highest-intent moment in the app. User has chosen a test and wants to book a time. A freeze here loses a customer who was ready to convert.

📄 Report Won't Load

The whole reason for using the app. Slow load = user opens WhatsApp PDF. Healthians loses the consultation upsell, rebook prompt, and future engagement.
